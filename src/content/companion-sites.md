The privacy policy at mindlynx.ai/privacy (updated 29 July 2026), in full substance:

- What is collected. Three things. What a visitor types into the contact form or a form Vera shows in chat: name, email, subject, message. And, only if a visitor chooses to talk to Vera, the conversation itself: messages go to Anthropic's Claude to generate replies, and in voice mode speech is transcribed by Deepgram and replies spoken by Cartesia, all for the duration of the conversation only. No transcripts are stored; the chat resets on leaving the page. Booking a call sends name, email and the chosen time to Calendly. No advertising trackers, no third-party analytics, no cross-site tracking.
- Cookies. One cookie, _tch_vid, a random identifier recognising a returning browser for up to a year, plus a session identifier _tch_sid that lives until the tab closes. Both first-party, never shared, containing nothing about who the visitor is. Browser cookie controls remove them; site preferences (like dismissing the cookie notice) are stored locally and never leave the browser.

The Helix and Albion announcement lists are consent only: an unticked box, a recorded timestamp, withdrawable any time by unsubscribing or emailing. Talking to Vera is entirely optional.

Anthropic's Claude generates Vera's replies; Deepgram and Cartesia handle voice in and out; Calendly holds call bookings. Each receives only what its job needs, only while the feature is used, and all act as processors under MindLynx's instructions. Details are never sold.
- Retention. Correspondence at most two years; waitlist entries until the product launches or the person unsubscribes.
- Rights. Under UK GDPR: a copy of what is held, correction, deletion, objection, consent withdrawal, all via hello@mindlynx.ai, and complaints to the Information Commissioner's Office.

ALBION.HELIX.WORK

The Albion site. Hero: Britain's sovereign compound model; one endpoint, many minds, and a receipt for every answer; keeps sovereign work sovereign, verifies instead of guessing, and distils what it proves into a British core you own. In development; the page itself says nothing on it is a live service claim. Three pillars: it shows its working (every answer carries a receipt: which model handled it, whether it stayed sovereign, what it cost), sovereign stays sovereign (fail-closed: a foreign or frontier model is refused the content before a single key is unlocked), and it verifies rather than guesses. Cost story: each task goes to the right-sized mind instead of paying the frontier premium, spend is itemised per answer, and what Albion proves distils into a core the customer owns, so the curve bends down. Sector editions planned for Legal, Financial Services, the Public Sector, Health and Defence; the sector named on the waitlist shapes the build order. The "why now" section points at the EU AI Act's dated audit duties and the UK's sovereign AI funding programme with an open procurement; the specific figures and dates are published on the page, so send people there rather than reciting them. Who it is for: UK and EU organisations first, public sector sovereignty mandates, legal and financial services under audit obligations, defence-adjacent suppliers. There is also a "cannot wait" route: a small number of four-week fixed-price builds, started with a scoping call.

The contributor register, as published: Albion is trained only on lawfully sourced, fully accounted-for knowledge. Contributors are paid properly and promptly (no scraping, no unpaid community labour dressed up as exposure), credited through an opt-in public register and the model card, keep the rights to their own expertise (they license the specific contribution and can reuse their knowledge anywhere), and share in the upside through a defined share of commercial revenue funding a contributor benefit pool. Open to British professionals with deep sector knowledge; the programme opens sector by sector and early registrants help shape it.

Data notes published on the Albion page: waitlist and register details are stored solely to manage those lists, never shared, kept until general release or 12 months after last contact, whichever is sooner, with erasure any time via hello@helix.work.
